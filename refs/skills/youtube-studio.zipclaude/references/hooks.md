# Hooks: 21 formulas and a scoring rubric

Use when: writing or fixing the first 5-30 seconds of a video or Short.

A hook has one job: make the right viewer decide "this is for me and I need the
next sentence." It is not a summary and not an introduction.

## Workflow

1. Write the video's promise in one sentence.
2. Pick 3-5 formulas below that fit the promise (not all 21).
3. Draft one hook per formula, using only facts you can back up.
4. Score each with the rubric. Present at least three, scored, with a recommendation.
5. Check the winner against the title and thumbnail: same promise, no bait.

Anything in `[brackets]` is a slot. Fill slots with real details from the user
or the research file. Never fill a number slot with a guess; use `[YOUR NUMBER]`.

## The 21 formulas

### 1. Result first
Template: Show the finished outcome, then say what it took and that you will show how.
Bad: "Today we're talking about [skill]."
Good: "This is [outcome]. It started as [messy starting point]. Here is what changed."

### 2. Mistake callout
Template: Name a specific mistake the viewer is likely making, then promise the fix.
Bad: "Many people struggle with [topic]."
Good: "If you [specific habit], you're quietly [specific cost]. Here's the fix."

### 3. Myth bust
Template: State the popular belief, then break it with one concrete reason.
Bad: "There are lots of myths about [topic]."
Good: "Everyone says [belief]. [One-line proof it fails]. Here's what holds up instead."

### 4. Direct question
Template: Ask the question the viewer already has in their head, in their words.
Bad: "Have you ever wondered about [topic]?"
Good: "Why does [familiar thing] keep [annoying result], no matter what you try?"

### 5. Cold open scene
Template: Start mid-moment with a vivid detail, then zoom out to why it matters.
Bad: "Let me tell you a story about [subject]."
Good: "[Time], [place]. [Something is about to go wrong]. This is where [topic] begins."

### 6. Challenge with stakes
Template: State the challenge, the rule, and what you lose if you fail.
Bad: "I'm going to try [thing]."
Good: "I'm doing [challenge] with [one strict rule]. If I fail, [real consequence]."

### 7. Hidden detail
Template: Point at something everyone sees and nobody notices, then promise to reveal it.
Bad: "There's something interesting about [topic]."
Good: "Look at [familiar object] again. There's a detail in it that explains [surprising effect]."

### 8. Before and after
Template: Contrast two states with a clear dividing action.
Bad: "I improved my [thing]."
Good: "Before: [bad state]. After: [good state]. The only difference is [one change]."

### 9. Real-number promise
Template: Use a number the user can prove, tied to a concrete outcome.
Bad: "Learn this in no time."
Good: "[YOUR NUMBER] steps, and by the end you'll [concrete result]." (number must be real)

### 10. Confession
Template: Admit a real failure, show what it cost, and promise the lesson.
Bad: "I made some mistakes along the way."
Good: "I got [specific thing] wrong for [real duration], and it cost me [real cost]."

### 11. Bold prediction
Template: Make a clear claim about what happens next and say why you believe it.
Bad: "The future of [topic] is interesting."
Good: "[Thing] will [specific change], and the evidence is already here: [one proof]."

### 12. Stop sign
Template: Tell the viewer to stop an action, give the reason in one beat.
Bad: "You might want to rethink [habit]."
Good: "Stop [specific action] right now. Here's what it's doing to [outcome they care about]."

### 13. Flipped assumption
Template: Say what the viewer assumes, then say the opposite is closer to true.
Bad: "It's not what you think."
Good: "You think [assumption]. Actually, [reversal], and [evidence] shows it."

### 14. Head-to-head
Template: Put two options in a fair test and say what decides the winner.
Bad: "Which is better, A or B?"
Good: "[A] versus [B], same [conditions], judged on [one clear measure]."

### 15. Time-boxed promise
Template: Promise a result inside a stated time that the video really fits.
Bad: "This will only take a bit."
Good: "In the next [real minutes], you'll be able to [specific ability]."

### 16. Insider access
Template: Offer a look at something normally hidden and say why you can show it.
Bad: "Here's some behind-the-scenes stuff."
Good: "This is what [process] looks like from the inside, because I [real reason you have access]."

### 17. Odd fact
Template: Lead with a surprising, verified fact, then connect it to the viewer.
Bad: "Fun fact about [topic]."
Good: "[Verified surprising fact]. That one detail is why [thing the viewer cares about]."

### 18. Shared frustration
Template: Name a specific annoyance the viewer hates, then side with them.
Bad: "Nobody likes [vague thing]."
Good: "You know that moment when [precise annoying moment]? It isn't your fault. Here's why."

### 19. Cliffhanger mini-story
Template: Tell two sentences of a story, stop at the turn, promise the rest later.
Bad: "I'll tell you what happened at the end."
Good: "I was [situation] when [turning point]. What I did next changed [outcome]; I'll show you."

### 20. Permission slip
Template: Remove a barrier the viewer believes in, then show the easy route.
Bad: "This is easy for anyone."
Good: "You don't need [expected expensive thing]. You need [simple alternative], and I'll show you."

### 21. Spoiler with a catch
Template: Give the answer upfront, then say the answer isn't the useful part.
Bad: "The answer is coming up."
Good: "The answer is [answer]. But the reason behind it is what actually changes [viewer outcome]."

## Scoring rubric (0-5, one point each)

| # | Test | Give the point if... |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Specific | It names a concrete outcome, object, moment, or person, not a vague topic. |
| 2 | Gap | It opens a question the viewer wants answered; they can't guess the answer. |
| 3 | Relevant | The target viewer sees themselves within the first two sentences. |
| 4 | Fast | The point arrives in the first 1-2 sentences; no greeting, no throat-clearing. |
| 5 | Honest | The video fully delivers what the hook implies, with no bait and no unverified claim. |

Caps: if "Honest" fails, the total can never exceed 2. If a number or claim is unverified, tag `[VERIFY]` and fail "Honest" until resolved.

Bands:
- **5** Ship it.
- **4** Ship after one tweak (say which test lost its point).
- **3** Rewrite with a different formula.
- **0-2** Discard.

## Presentation template (use every time)

```
Hook A  [formula #]  Score 4/5  (Specific 1, Gap 1, Relevant 1, Fast 1, Honest 0 - needs source)
"..."
Hook B  [formula #]  Score 5/5  ...
Hook C  [formula #]  Score 3/5  ...
Recommend: B, because [one reason tied to the rubric].
```

## Hook rules of thumb

- Greeting, channel name, and "before we start" all come after the hook, not in it.
- Cut every word before the first interesting word.
- Say it the way the creator speaks (voice.md), not the way a poster reads.
- The hook's visual does half the work: write what the viewer sees on line one.
- Re-test the hook after the body is written; the best hook is often hiding in section two.

## Hook checklist

- [ ] At least three options, each labelled with a formula number
- [ ] Each scored with all five tests, not just a total
- [ ] No fabricated number, quote, or source (placeholders used)
- [ ] The recommended hook scores 4 or 5
- [ ] Hook, title and thumbnail all promise the same thing
- [ ] First visual is described, not left to chance
