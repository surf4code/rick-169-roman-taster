# Voice: capture a creator's voice from three samples

Use when: the script must sound like a particular creator (usually the user), or
when a draft sounds generic and needs personality.

Goal: write a short **voice profile** from three real samples, then check drafts
against it. Capture habits and traits, not sentences.

## 1. Inputs

- Three samples of the creator's real speech or writing: transcripts of three
  videos, or pasted scripts. Ask for transcripts if the user only gives links
  and you cannot read them. Never guess a voice from a channel name.
- Choose samples that show range: a favorite, a typical one, and one on a
  different topic or format. Even a few minutes from each is enough to start.
- Optional: one line from the creator about how they want to sound ("calm,"
  "funny," "direct").

## 2. What to look for (per sample)

| Dimension | Questions to ask |
|---|---|
| Openings | How do they start? A question, a claim, a scene? Any greeting habit? |
| Sentence length | Short and punchy, long and flowing, or mixed? |
| Rhythm | Where do they pause, stack, or repeat? Any lists of three? |
| Vocabulary | Casual or formal? Jargon explained or assumed? Favorite words? |
| Humor | Dry, self-teasing, playful, none? How often? |
| Address | "You," "we," "I"? Do they talk down, across, or up? |
| Stance | Confident, hedging, skeptical, encouraging? |
| Transitions | How do they move between ideas? Any signature bridge? |
| Evidence style | Stories, numbers, demos, analogies, quotes? |
| Language mix | One language or a blend? Which parts switch and why? |
| Energy | Calm, high, shifts by section? |
| Never-say list | Words or moves they avoid |

## 3. Compare across the three

1. Mark a trait **signature** if it shows up in at least two samples.
2. Mark a trait **situational** if it appears in only one (note when).
3. List 3-5 phrases the creator genuinely repeats; they may be reused sparingly.
4. Note one thing the creator never does.
5. If samples contradict, ask the creator which one feels more like them.

## 4. Voice profile template

```
VOICE PROFILE: [creator / channel]
Built from: [3 samples, titles or labels]

One-line summary: (e.g., "friendly expert who explains by example")
Pace and sentence shape:
Vocabulary level and jargon rule:
Language mix and how it's written:
Humor (type, frequency):
How they address the viewer:
Stance and energy:
Typical opening moves:
Typical transitions:
Evidence they like (stories / demos / analogies / numbers):
Signature phrases (max 5, use sparingly):
Never do:
Sample "in voice" sentence:
Sample "out of voice" sentence:
Notes / open questions for the creator:
```

## 5. Applying the profile

- Write the draft, then do a voice pass: read each paragraph aloud in your head as the creator.
- Replace formal or generic phrasing with the creator's own habits.
- Keep sentence length and pause rhythm close to the profile.
- Use signature phrases at most once or twice; overuse becomes parody.
- Facts and numbers still follow the hard rules in SKILL.md, whatever the voice.

## 6. Voice test

Take one paragraph of the draft.
1. Mark every phrase that does not sound like the creator.
2. Rewrite those phrases using the profile.
3. Ask the creator: "Would you say this out loud?" Update the profile with their answer.

A "swap test" also helps: if the paragraph could be read unchanged by a different creator, it needs more of this one's habits.

## 7. Ethics and limits

- Match style traits only. Do not copy long verbatim lines from any creator's videos.
- If the profile is of someone other than the user, use it as a style reference. Do not write words attributed to a real person that they did not say, and do not use a voice to mislead viewers about who is speaking.
- Do not store private details from samples beyond what the voice work needs.
- Keep the profile short and editable; update it after a few new videos.

## Voice checklist

- [ ] Three real samples received (not invented or inferred from a name)
- [ ] Traits split into signature vs situational
- [ ] Profile filled with a sample in-voice and out-of-voice sentence
- [ ] Draft passed the aloud and swap tests
- [ ] Signature phrases used sparingly
- [ ] No long verbatim lines from the samples copied into the script
