# Research: shock scores, source checks, notes to beats

Use when: gathering facts for a script, checking claims, or turning a transcript
or raw notes into a beat outline.

Core rule: no invented numbers, quotes, or sources. If you cannot check something
(no browsing, no document), say so, tag it `[VERIFY]`, and ask the user for a
source. A weaker verified claim beats a stronger unverified one.

## 1. Research brief (fill first, keep short)

```
Question the video answers:
Viewer and what they already know:
The claim I expect to make:
What evidence would change my mind:
Sources the user already has (links, notes, files):
```

## 2. Shock-score method

Goal: decide which facts deserve screen time and which can lead a hook.
Score every candidate fact from 1-5 on four tests.

| Test | 1 means | 5 means |
|---|---|---|
| Surprise | Viewer already knows it | Viewer would repeat it to a friend |
| Stakes | Doesn't affect them | Changes a decision, a budget, or a belief |
| Proof | One weak or unclear source | Primary source, or two independent sources agree |
| Showable | Hard to illustrate | Easy to put on screen (document, demo, footage, chart) |

Shock score = sum of the four (max 20). Record in a table:

| Fact (one line) | Surprise | Stakes | Proof | Showable | Total | Use as |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Rules for using the scores:
- Proof 1-2: do not state as fact. Reword as "reportedly" or drop.
- A hook fact needs Surprise 4+ and Proof 4+.
- Totals of 15+ are lead candidates; 10-14 are support beats; under 10 are cut.
- Keep the 3-5 strongest facts and spread them through the video, not stacked up front.
- Re-score after checking sources; scores often drop.

## 3. Source-check rules

1. **Use primary sources** when possible (original document, filing, dataset, interview, official page). Treat summaries as pointers, not proof.
2. **Two independent sources** for any surprising claim. Copies of the same article do not count twice.
3. **Check the date.** Is the fact still current? Say "as of [date]" when it could change.
4. **Trace numbers.** Find where a figure first came from; check units, scope, and who measured it.
5. **Quote exactly or paraphrase fully.** Never reword a quote and keep the quotation marks. Keep direct quotes short and credit them.
6. **Context check.** Does the claim still hold when you read the sentences around it?
7. **Check the incentive.** Who gains if you believe this? Note it if it matters.
8. **Mark confidence in the script:** confirmed, reported, disputed, unknown. Use the matching word on camera.
9. **Never name a source you have not seen.** No invented studies, experts, books, or links.
10. **If sources disagree,** show the disagreement or pick the stronger source and say why.
11. **No browsing available?** Tell the user. Work only from what they gave you, and list what needs verifying.

Source log (keep for the description and for corrections):

| # | Claim | Source (title, author, date) | Link/where | Type (primary/secondary) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## 4. Turning a transcript or notes into beats

Input can be an interview transcript, a recorded ramble, meeting notes, or a
research dump. Work in this order.

1. **Clean.** Remove filler and repeats. Keep a copy of the original.
2. **Highlight.** Mark every moment that is a claim, a surprise, a story, an example, or a quote worth keeping.
3. **Name each highlight** with a short label (five words or fewer).
4. **Cluster.** Group labels that serve the same point. Each group is a candidate beat.
5. **Score** the facts in each group (section 2). Drop clusters that score low.
6. **Order** the beats by the chosen structure (structures.md). Put the strongest early or at the climax.
7. **Write a beat card** for each (purpose, claim, proof, visual, exit line).
8. **Find the gaps.** Where is a claim without proof or a beat without a visual? List them.
9. **Rewrite in the creator's voice** (voice.md), in your own words, not the transcript's.

Beat extraction template:

```
Highlight: "short label"
Type: claim / story / example / quote / stat
Source location: (timestamp, page, or note id)
Beat it feeds:
Needs verifying? yes / no
```

Rules for handling source material:
- Summarize in your own words. Do not paste long passages into the script.
- Keep direct quotes short, exact, attributed, and used sparingly.
- Material from other people's videos or articles is research input, not script text.
- If the user supplied their own notes, you may stay close to their meaning; preserve their stories and examples.

## 5. Research output (hand back)

- A shock-score table (top 5 facts)
- A source log
- A `[VERIFY]` list of anything unconfirmed
- A beat list ready for structures.md

## Research checklist

- [ ] Brief filled in, including "what would change my mind"
- [ ] Every fact scored; weak-proof facts downgraded or cut
- [ ] Surprising claims have two sources or are labelled
- [ ] Dates checked; "as of" added where needed
- [ ] No fabricated source, number, or quote anywhere
- [ ] Quotes short and exact; everything else in original words
- [ ] Source log complete; `[VERIFY]` items listed for the user
