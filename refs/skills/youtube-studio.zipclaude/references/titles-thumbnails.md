# Titles and thumbnails

Use when: naming a video, designing its thumbnail, or fixing weak click rates.

Packaging (title + thumbnail) earns the click; the video earns the watch. Both
must promise the same thing, and the video must deliver it. Never promise what
the video does not contain.

## 1. Title formulas

Fill each with real details. Write 8-10 candidates, then shortlist three.

| # | Formula | Template |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Outcome + method | How to [result] with [method] |
| 2 | Mistake | [Number] [Topic] Mistakes That [cost] (only if the count is real) |
| 3 | Question | Why Does [thing] [surprising result]? |
| 4 | Challenge | I [did hard thing] and [what happened] |
| 5 | Myth | [Popular belief] Is Wrong. Here's Why |
| 6 | Versus | [A] vs [B]: [what decides it] |
| 7 | Secret | The [adjective] Detail Behind [familiar thing] |
| 8 | Beginner path | [Skill] From Zero: What To Learn First |
| 9 | Story | The [person/thing] That [unexpected outcome] |
| 10 | Warning | Don't [action] Until You Know This |
| 11 | Result stack | [Outcome] Without [common pain] |
| 12 | Explainer | [Topic] Explained Simply |
| 13 | Ranking | [Items] Ranked: [criteria] |
| 14 | Before you start | What I Wish I Knew Before [Doing X] |
| 15 | Year-aware | [Topic] in [current year]: What Changed |

Title rules:
- Put the main keyword and the main promise early; mobile screens may cut the end.
- Rule of thumb: stay around 60 characters or fewer so it is less likely to be truncated. Check how it looks on a phone.
- Use plain words a stranger would search for; avoid private jokes and in-house terms.
- Numbers only when the count is true and the video delivers all of them.
- Do not repeat the thumbnail text word for word; they should work together.
- Honesty test: if a viewer read only the title, would the video match what they expect?

Always give a **set of three** titles with a recommendation and one-line reason.

## 2. Three thumbnail concepts

Offer three different directions, not three variations of one idea.

```
Option A - Face + emotion
  Subject: close-up face showing [specific emotion]
  Text: [up to 4 words]
  Focal point: eyes or the object they react to
  Colors: [two high-contrast colors]
  Why it gets clicked:

Option B - Object or contrast
  Subject: one clear object, or a before/after split
  Text: [up to 4 words, or none]
  Focal point:
  Colors:
  Why it gets clicked:

Option C - Diagram or text-led
  Subject: a simple shape, arrow, or chart showing the idea
  Text: [up to 4 words]
  Focal point:
  Colors:
  Why it gets clicked:

Recommend: [A/B/C] because [reason tied to the viewer and the title].
```

## 3. Thumbnail rules (judged at 120x68 pixels)

At 120x68 the thumbnail is tiny. Design for that size first.

- Shrink the design to 120x68. If you cannot tell what it is in one second, simplify.
- One focal point only. One subject, one idea.
- Text: four words or fewer, thick, high-contrast, large enough to read at that size.
- Keep important elements away from the bottom-right corner, where the duration badge sits.
- Strong contrast between subject, text, and background; avoid busy backgrounds.
- Faces work when the emotion is real and readable; do not use them just to have a face.
- Limit to two or three main colors. Avoid colors that blend into the app's white or dark page.
- No tiny logos, small labels, or paragraphs.
- Do not fake events or show things the video never contains.
- Look at it beside similar videos: does it stand out, or does it blend in?

## 4. CTR checklist (before publishing)

- [ ] Title promise, thumbnail promise, and hook promise are the same
- [ ] Title understandable in three seconds, no insider terms
- [ ] Main keyword appears near the start of the title
- [ ] Thumbnail has one focal point and four or fewer words
- [ ] Readable at 120x68; checked on a phone-sized view
- [ ] Not copying the text of the title; adds a second angle
- [ ] Contrast check passes in both light and dark mode of the app
- [ ] Looks different from the user's last few thumbnails and from direct competitors
- [ ] Honest: the video fully delivers on everything shown and said
- [ ] A backup title and thumbnail ready in case the first underperforms

## 5. After publishing

If the video's click rate lags the creator's own recent average, change the title
first (cheap, fast), then the thumbnail. Change one at a time and note the date.
YouTube has offered a built-in way to test packaging variations in Studio; verify
what is currently available before recommending it. See analytics-review.md.
Do not quote benchmark click rates; compare with the channel's own history.
