# What makes these shorts work

## Shape

- 9:16 vertical, 45-70 s, usually around 60.
- 15-25 shots, with a new shot every 2-4 s. Each shot is one narration line, shown literally.
- One narrator tells the story in the present tense, casually, like telling a friend something wild. They voice other characters' lines inside quotes.
- Captions: 1-2 words at a time, big bold white text with a black outline, a bit below the centre of the frame.
- Music sits quietly under the voice. Every action has its own sound effect.
- The video ends on the last word of the story. No "subscribe", no outro.

## Story beats (most strong scripts follow these, in order)

1. **Hook (0-3 s).** Drop the viewer into the middle of the strangest concrete moment. Show it in the frame.
2. **Context.** Often opened with "You see, …". Who, where, what's at stake.
3. **Doubters.** Someone laughs, refuses, or yells at the hero, ideally in a quoted line.
4. **Escalation in concrete steps.** Numbers, dates and counts: "on day 3… by day 30…", "the fifth time this year", "0.4 seconds".
5. **Turn (around 40-45 s).** "Then one night…", "Until…", "But…".
6. **Mechanism.** A second "You see, …" that explains why it works, through an everyday analogy.
7. **Payoff.** The last line lands the consequence, usually on the doubters.

You don't rewrite the user's story into this shape. Use it only to judge the hook and to plan the visuals: give the payoff and the turn the strongest shots.

## Hooks

A good hook is a **concrete, strange action with a specific detail**, taken from this story:
- "A hiring bot rejected Leonardo da Vinci in 0.4 seconds."
- "She mailed herself to Paris in a wooden crate."
- "The town's only traffic light was controlled by a goat."

Weak hooks set the scene instead of acting: "When X happened, things got weird." Strengthen such a hook by moving the story's most absurd concrete moment, or its sharpest number, to the front. You may also open on the final image and let the video explain how we got there.

Never invent facts or events. Always show the user the original and the sharpened hook, and let them choose.

The hook **picture** matters as much as the words. Pick the most striking visual in the whole story for shot 1. Make sure it says *where/who* at a glance: a road sign, a costume, a landmark.

## Visual language

- **Characters:** a big white ball head (2D: a flat egg with a thick outline; 3D: a smooth matte ball) with a simple drawn face. Thin dark stick arms and legs. Recognisability comes from hair, headwear, glasses, beard, clothes, colours and accessories, not from facial features.
- **Emotion** is shown big and cartoony: eyes into circles, eyebrows flying up, a sweat drop, a single tear, a face turning red or purple, steam from the head.
- **World:** a stylised 3D set, simplified, rich colour, soft cinematic light, real depth. One clear light mood per location: candlelit night, sunny office, cold blue server hall.
- **Jokes live in props:** a stamp that says REJECTED, a calendar flipping days, a newspaper headline, a sign on a door. Keep the words short and spell them exactly.
- **Explaining mechanisms:** cross-sections, top-down views, a close-up on the one detail that matters, a split screen.
- **Camera:** always moving with purpose (slow push-ins, orbits, tracking along an object, a crane up on a reveal). A static frame reads as cheap. The exception is shots where props must not move: lock the camera and say so.
- **Continuity:** the same room looks the same every time it appears, with the same props in the same places. Use the location's first keyframe as a reference for all its later shots.
