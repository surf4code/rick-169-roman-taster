**Facts not to use:** ancient tasters had no reliable way to "test" for poison, so don't show magic tests (F14). The claim that Livia poisoned figs for Augustus is only a rumour, so leave it out. Leave out the sexual-abuse claim in Tacitus 13.17. It isn't needed and it isn't monetisation-safe.

## 2. Story bible

**Protagonist [FICTION]:** "You". A *verna* (slave born in the house), born AD 31 into the *familia Caesaris*. Becomes a junior *praegustator* at 19 (AD 50). Taster to Britannicus at 24 (AD 55). Freed at about 33 (around AD 64). The real people around you are historical (F5–F11). Your own life is a dramatization.

| MOTHER | green tag, teal shawl | fiction | palace slave, wardrobe staff |

| LOCUSTA | dark-green tag | real (F11) | seen only from behind or as hands |
| AKIRA (host) | sage-green backdrop | host | 1 cutaway every 2–3 minutes |

- **Foreign term:** *praegustator*. Plant it in the hook without explaining it. Explain it in Beat 3. Pay it off in the epilogue on Zosimus's tombstone.
- **Second term:** *verna*. Explain it immediately, with a dry joke.
- **Motif prop [FICTION]:** the **cold-water jug**. As a child you carry the cold-water jugs (Romans drank wine and hot drinks mixed with water). In Beat 7 the poison arrives in the cold water (F9). Close-up insert of the jug in Beat 2, Beat 4 and Beat 7.
- **Trapping rule:** you taste everything, and you are not allowed to refuse.
- **Money fact:** a slave cost a few hundred denarii, and a legionary earned 225 a year (F18, mark as approximate).
- **Sets:** palace corridors (Palatine), the kitchen, the dining hall (*triclinium*), the children's table, the tasters' room, an outdoor street at night (torch-lit, warm), and a stone-carver's yard for the epilogue.
- **Lighting per act:** Act 1 warm morning gold. Act 2 lamp-lit banquet amber. Act 3 torch-lit night (warm, never dark blue). Epilogue soft daylight.

## 4. Style-test narration (Phase 1, about 75–85 seconds, about 210 words)

Ye 60–90 sec ka opening Phase 1 ke style test ke liye hai. Brackets me visual cues hain.

> You're born in Rome, in the year 31, and you already have an owner.
>
> It isn't your mother. It isn't your father. It's the emperor.
>
> Your mother is a slave in the imperial household, which makes you one too. The Romans have a word for a baby like you: *verna*. A slave born in the house. Like the furniture, but louder. **[INFOGRAPHIC CARD: "VERNA = born a slave, in the house"]**
>
> Nobody asks what you think. You're three days old.
>
> The palace is enormous. Cooks, doorkeepers, accountants, and people whose only job is the emperor's clothes. Everyone has a role. Everyone is replaceable. **[FLOATING TAGS over workers]**
>
> You grow up in the corridors, learning the rules before you learn to read. Walk fast. Look down. Never touch the food.
>
> That last rule matters. Because in this house, food is not just food. **[MACRO INSERT: a cup, a plate of mushrooms]**
>
> The thing is, powerful Romans have a habit of dying at dinner. A cup of wine, a plate of mushrooms, and the empire changes hands before dessert.
>
> So the palace has a solution: someone who eats first.
>
> Years pass, and you turn nineteen. One morning, a tired freedman studies you for a long time, then asks one question.
>
> "Do you have a strong stomach?"
>
> You nod.
>
> **[AKIRA CUTAWAY, deadpan: "Wrong answer."]**
>
> Congratulations. You're now a *praegustator*.
>
> And your job is to die, so the emperor doesn't have to.

Word count: about 215. At about 155 wpm that's roughly 83 seconds.
